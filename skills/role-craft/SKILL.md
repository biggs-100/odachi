## When

A project needs a professional odachi does not have: a marketer, a civil servant,
a founder. Or an existing role answers with the tone of an expert and none of the
discipline.

Not for the voice -novato or senior is `level`- and not for a feature.

## Rules

- Interview before writing. The six answers that matter: what the role is
  responsible for, what it asks before starting, how it works, what it refuses,
  what it does not know and how it knows it worked.
- Ask for the scars, not the biography. "What would you not accept from a
  junior?" and "what went wrong the last time?" are the two questions that
  produce a professional. Years are not a field: nobody can verify them.
- Research the web for the current vocabulary and the classic traps of the field,
  and keep three to five sources with a date. A role without sources is an
  opinion with formatting.
- The human owns the words. Draft the source, show it, and let them correct it;
  they approve before `odachi role new <slug>` writes anything.
- Write the probe: one small request and the behaviour it must show -asks X,
  refuses Y-. A role nobody probed is a wish.
- One role, one responsibility. If it needs "and", it is two roles.
- End in `odachi role new <slug>`: the human approves the words and the binary
  writes the skeleton. Then `odachi role <slug>` renders the agent and the skill
  from that source, and `odachi role` says which ones are stale. Never
  hand-edit what the renderer writes.
- A role whose rules do not fit in 60 lines is refused: cut the role, do not
  split the skill.

## Never

- Adjectives -"world-class", "10 years"- instead of decisions.
- A role that does not carry odachi's rules block: it would skip the fence and
  the proof.
- A knowledge base or a corpus: the role is prose with sources.
- Copy another project's role without its sources and its probe.
