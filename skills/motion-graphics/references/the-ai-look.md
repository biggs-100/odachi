# The machine-made look

Generated motion fails in a small number of ways, and they repeat. None of them is a style choice: each
one is a default that was never decided.

## The catalogue

| the tell | why it reads machine-made | the fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the same ease on every tween | one adverb for a whole piece: nothing has its own character | name the adverb per scene; at most two tweens share an ease |
| the same duration on everything | uniform weight; nothing lands, nothing is urgent | choose the band from the element's weight, 3x between slowest and fastest |
| `y: 30, opacity: 0` on every entrance | every element is the same object arriving the same way | enter from the direction the layout implies: a left column from the left, a card from its own edge, a number by counting |
| nothing starts at t=0 | a cut that begins the instant the file begins feels like a jump cut | offset the first beat 0.1-0.3 s; let the frame exist before the motion |
| centered text floating on an empty background | the web habit: a page has margins, a frame has edges | anchor to an edge or split the frame; two focal points and three layers |
| the gradient is the design | a gradient is a background, not a composition | structure the ground: panels, rules, an oversized faded word bleeding off-frame |
| ambient zoom in every scene | ambient motion everywhere cancels itself | pick one scene to breathe; hold in the others |
| a uniform grid of equal cards | equal weight is no hierarchy | one card wins by size, crop or position; the rest support it |
| a shine or a sweep on the logo | the reveal says nothing the logo did not already say | give the mark a job: build it, assemble it from the product, let it resolve the piece |
| a whoosh on every transition | sound as decoration adds cost and no meaning | punctuate the beat that matters; silence is a sound |
| a shadow or a glow on everything | several light sources and depths in one frame | one light direction, one elevation per level |
| perfect symmetry | it reads as a diagram | asymmetry: one heavy side, one breathing side |
| text over busy footage with no scrim | contrast changes frame to frame | a plate, a scrim or a different crop under the text, always |
| four families and six weights | the type is doing the decorating | one family, two weights, one accent at most |
| word-by-word kinetic type for a paragraph | it is unreadable at 1x and exhausting at arm's length | one phrase at a time, 3-6 words, 1.5 s minimum |
| a number that appears without resolving | a count-up that stops on a wrong value, or a value with no unit | land on the real value, hold it, carry its unit and its "as of" |
| a loop with a jump | first and last frame do not match | design the seam: same position, same light, or a deliberate cut |
| a fade to black between scenes | black means over | change the frame, not the universe |
| every scene ends the same way | variety was mistaken for rhythm | one transition per register change, chosen for meaning |
| everything moves at the same time | the eye cannot rank what it sees | one lead per beat, everything else later or smaller |
| the stock "corporate" palette | the piece could belong to anyone | one accent that carries the brand, a neutral ramp tinted with it |

## The three that survive review

Most of the table is caught by a person in four seconds. These three survive a whole review, because
they look deliberate:

- **Motion that explains nothing.** A decorative sweep, a line that draws to nowhere, a particle field
  behind a number. The test: remove it and the message survives — then it is decoration. Keep it only
  if it is the piece's identity, and then give it the whole piece.
- **One idea split into three scenes.** The piece says the same thing three times with different
  layouts, which reads as padding. The fix is a shorter piece, not more scenes.
- **The landing that arrives on the wrong frame.** Nine tenths of the work is the entrance, and the
  landing —the number at rest, the word at full size, the logo settled— is cut at the moment it
  completes. A landing needs 0.3-0.5 s of stillness after it, or the viewer never sees the result.

## The aftertaste test

Watch the piece once, look away, and say what it said. If the answer is the visual style —"it had nice
transitions"— the piece is a demo. If the answer is the idea, the motion did its job and should be
invisible in the retelling.

That is the target: motion a senior designer would call inevitable, not impressive.
