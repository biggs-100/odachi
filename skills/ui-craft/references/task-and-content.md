# Task and content

## The first screen is the job

A screen is chosen by what the person comes to do, not by a template. A product has several jobs; find the
leading one for this surface and build that one first.

| the job | give priority to | the useful outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| find | search scope, filters, recognizable results | reaching the item without inspecting every entry |
| compare | the attributes that decide, in a stable order | understanding the difference before choosing |
| act | the current state, the primary action, its consequence | finishing the operation without losing context |
| read | a heading that names the place, readable text, a way back | understanding, and finding the passage again |
| decide | the proposition, the evidence, the next step | choosing with the information there is |

- A work surface puts its controls and its current material in the first viewport. A promotional hero ahead
  of an existing user's records costs a click every day.
- Navigation follows the objects people recognize, not the org chart: "Reservations, Tools, Members", never
  "Operations → Resource management".
- The main action sits next to its object: "Reserve tool" belongs with availability, not in settings.
- An irreversible action shows its consequence before it commits.
- One primary action per view. The alternatives stay visible and secondary.

## Real content, or none

- Design with the worst content: the longest name, the biggest number, an unbreakable email, zero items.
- Never invent a customer, a quote, a metric or a chart. An invented testimonial is a lie with a layout.
- Demonstration data is labelled as such, in the interface, not only in the handoff.
- If there is no evidence, say the capability plainly. "Used by 10,000 teams" with no number behind it is
  the fastest way to lose the reader who checks.

## The label predicts the outcome

| the weak label | better, when it is true | why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Continue` | `Review reservation` | names the next stage without promising the end |
| `Manage` | `Change pickup time` | says which operation is on offer |
| `Something went wrong` | `Reservation not saved. Try again.` | states the consequence and the recovery |
| `The future of shared spaces` | `Book workshop tools for your next project` | names the audience's activity |

A generic label forces the person to open the thing to find out what it does. A specific one makes the
choice legible before the click.

## The visual idea comes from the subject

- Take a cue from the thing being designed and use it across the surface, not in the hero only: a workshop
  booking page can be a calm timetable with availability legible at a glance.
- The palette, the type and the shapes come from the project's tokens. A direction is decisions about
  emphasis, content and layout, not the absence of shadows.
- Inventory what exists before deciding the page needs imagery. A real diagram or document explains the
  product; a stock photo only decorates the claim.
- Sections may change rhythm when the subject changes; comparable options keep one structure. Neither a
  different layout per section nor identical blocks everywhere is a rule.

## What this file does not decide

The hierarchy and the numbers are in `the-numbers.md`, the model's tells in `the-tells.md`, and the copy of
each state in `states-and-copy.md`. This file decides what the screen is for and what it says.
