# A direction, chosen and named

The brief says who and what for. A direction says how it looks and behaves, and it is
a decision, not a taste: three directions that differ in strategy, one chosen, its
tradeoff named in a sentence. A screen with no named direction defaults to the one
every model reaches for -the illustration, the gradient and the three equal cards- and
that default is four years old.

## Four postures, and the axis each one moves

| axis | one end | the other end | what it costs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| density | calm and dense: more per screen, less ornament | airy: one idea per viewport | dense loses the beginner; airy loses the expert |
| voice | editorial: a display serif, real paragraph measure | utilitarian: one grotesque, tight scale | editorial needs better copy; utilitarian reads as a tool |
| colour | muted and earthy: one accent, tinted neutrals | high contrast: wide-gamut accent, hard edges | muted disappears in a demo; loud fatigues in a session |
| depth | flat: borders and space do the work | material: translucency, elevation, light | flat needs hierarchy from type; material needs a reason |

A direction picks one point per axis and says why. "Dense, editorial, muted, flat" is
a direction. "Modern and clean" is not: it names no axis and therefore decides nothing.

## Three directions, not three colours

Write three, and make them differ in **structure or strategy**, never in the accent
colour or the corner radius:

1. **The one the job asks for**: the layout that follows the task most literally.
2. **The one the subject earns**: the metaphor the content itself supplies -a ledger
   looks like a ledger, a booking tool looks like a timetable.
3. **The one the audience expects from a competitor**, stated as the safe baseline.

Each carries one sentence of tradeoff: what it makes easy and what it gives up. The
human picks; the agent does not pick the one it likes.

## The default when the brief is silent

Dense and calm, one grotesque with an editorial display size, one accent tinted through
the neutrals, flat with one elevation reserved for what actually floats. That is the
posture of the products that look current in 2026 -Linear, Stripe, Vercel- and it is a
default, not a house style: a wedding invitation and a trading terminal both deserve
their own.

## Steal the principle, never the layout

Copying a screenshot copies its content's proportions and its subject. What transfers
is the principle: "the number is the headline", "the navigation is one row because the
work is the table", "the empty state is an invitation". Name the principle you took and
what you changed, in the delivery. A design that quotes another product's layout is the
fastest way to look like last year with somebody else's font.

## What makes it feel current, and what only looks current

- Current: the interface does something -it streams, it composes, it answers a
  command, it shows its work- and the density is earned by the task.
- Only looks current: glass over a hero, a gradient on the text, a pill for every
  label, a grid of cards that could hold anything. `the-tells.md` is the list, and a
  direction that needs one of those rows is a direction that has not been decided.

## What this page does not decide

The scale, the contrast and the vector of the layout are `the-numbers.md`'s. The
artboards and the manifest are `design`'s. The job the screen serves, and the words it
uses, are `task-and-content.md`'s. The motion posture is `motion-identity.md`'s.
