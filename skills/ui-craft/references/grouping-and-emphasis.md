# Grouping and emphasis

A screen that is read in the right order is designed; one that is only looked at is
decorated. Nothing here adds a number -the scale and the spacing are `the-numbers.md`'s-
and nothing here chooses a state: `states-and-copy.md` does.

## Proximity before the container

- Related things sit closer to each other than to unrelated ones. That is the
  cheapest grouping there is, and it survives every breakpoint.
- A heading and an alignment say "these belong together" before a border does. Reach
  for the container when the relationship needs to be visible when the screen is
  scanned quickly, not because a group exists.
- One container per meaning: a card per row, and then a border per label, value and
  action inside it, is a screen that cannot be scanned. A nested boundary earns its
  place when it separates a distinct interactive region or changes how the content is
  read.
- A border, a surface or a shadow reveals a real boundary or an elevation. When
  everything has one, none of them says anything.

## Emphasis is scarce

- One element leads per screen or per region: the current task, or the heading. When
  every line is bold and oversized, the reader has to read everything to find
  anything.
- The roles are title, section heading, body and label, and each is distinct by the
  scale in `the-numbers.md`, not by a new font. A trend is not a reason to replace the
  project's typeface.
- Comparable numbers align: the same left edge, the same decimal and the same unit,
  or `9`, `12` and `105` stop being comparable. A heading wraps when it needs to
  rather than being clipped to a fixed number of lines.
- Every word in a pill, a badge or an icon removes a word from the page: use them for
  state or for recognition, and keep the label when the icon alone is ambiguous.

## Density follows frequency and consequence

- Repeated expert work wants compact rows. Infrequent or consequential choices want
  the space that lets a person decide, and that space is not decoration.
- A metric earns its tile when it changes a decision, and it says its scope and its
  freshness. A row of invented trend tiles pushes the task off the screen to answer no
  question.
- What is selected, disabled, hovered and focused are four different looks, and focus
  must stay visible against whatever is behind it.
- When a control is unavailable and the reason is not obvious, say why beside it and
  give the path that resolves it. A tooltip is not where a person looks.
