# Peru's AI rules for office work: what is in force in November 2026

This document maps the Peruvian legal and regulatory framework that governs how a public servant may use artificial intelligence to produce documents, research and reports. It is written for the person who drafts and signs the document, not for the person who builds the system. The conclusion is that Peru has a promotion law plus a binding regulation with real duties: an office worker cannot treat AI output as verified fact, cannot paste personal data into a public tool, and cannot let a system decide on people. The evidence habits odachi already teaches — written intent, observable acceptance, a proof tied to the exact tree, and a named reviewer — fit these duties directly.

Status of this research: verified 2026-11-23 against primary texts on `busquedas.elperuano.pe`, `gob.pe` and the Congress site. Items marked **UNVERIFIED** in this document were confirmed only through official listings or secondary sources and must not be cited as settled law.

## Quick path

1. **Check your entity's policy and officer first.** Every public entity needs an institutional AI policy and an Oficial de Inteligencia Artificial (OIA). Ask for both before changing how you write a report.
2. **Never paste personal or reserved data into a public AI tool.** Ley 29733 and the AI regulation's privacy principle apply to the prompt, not only to the published document.
3. **Treat every number and citation as unverified until you check the primary source.** Record the source you checked; that record is the evidence.
4. **Label public-facing AI-generated content.** The regulation exempts internal administrative support that does not affect rights or services — know which side your document is on.
5. **Do not let AI decide on people.** Selection, benefits, credit, health, discipline and similar decisions are high-risk uses, not drafting aids.

## 1. The binding regulation: DS 115-2025-PCM

The regulation is the operative text for an office worker. It has six titles and 36 articles. Its scope covers public administration, state enterprises under FONAFE, regional and local public companies, private organisations, civil society and academia. Personal use and defence or national-security uses that meet listed principles are excluded (arts. 3-4).

### Three risk classes

| Class | Rule | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Uso indebido — prohibited** (art. 23) | Cannot be used under any circumstance. | Deceptive or manipulative influence; autonomous lethal capacity; mass surveillance without legal basis; biometric inference of race, politics, religion or sexuality; real-time biometric categorisation in public spaces (narrow exceptions for digital-identity authentication and listed serious crimes); crime prediction from personality profiling. |
| **Riesgo alto** (art. 24) | Allowed only under strict conditions and controls. | Critical infrastructure; education assessment; worker selection, contracting and dismissal; access to social programmes; credit decisions except fraud detection; health access and triage; emotion inference at work or school. |
| **Aceptable** (art. 22.2) | Everything else, still subject to the whole regulation. | Drafting, summarising and researching documents with human review. |

For office work the boundary is simple: drafting a report is acceptable risk; using a model to rank job applicants or to decide who receives a benefit is high risk and is not a drafting task.

### Transparency and privacy

- Article 25 requires visible labelling when AI produces public-facing content, with an express exception for AI that supports internal administrative processes with no direct impact on decisions affecting rights or services. Where a decision affects human rights, the affected person must receive an explanation in accessible language.
- Article 26 submits all AI development and use to the personal-data regime and places responsibility for breaches under Ley 29733 and its regulation.
- Article 7.d requires respect for copyright and related rights; article 7.h requires reliability, human supervision and proportionality.

### Duties of public entities

Article 28 requires an institutional policy for safe, responsible and ethical AI use; use of NTP-ISO/IEC 42001:2025; multidisciplinary teams; evidence of public problems; staff training; AI projects in the Plan de Gobierno Digital; and compliance with the Public Function Code of Ethics. Article 29 adds risk management, privacy by design, security audits before deployment and during operation, and incident management. Article 30 requires a high-risk impact assessment before development or implementation. Article 34.1 sends non-compliance reports to the Contraloría General; article 36 opens a citizen alert channel at `gob.pe/iaperu`.

The phasing in the first final provision has already run for the Poder Ejecutivo, Legislativo, Judicial and autonomous constitutional bodies (one year from 2025-09-09). EsSalud, regional governments and public universities had two years; local governments type A, B and C had three; types D to G are optional.

## 2. ENIA 2026-2030 and the AI officer

RM 152-2026-PCM makes the ENIA mandatory for public entities and state enterprises. It requires a Plan de Acción de IA of at least three years, with diagnosis, objectives, indicators, schedule and resources, validated by the Comité de Gobierno y Transformación Digital and approved by the entity's top authority. The OIA must be designated before the plan. Entities report quarterly to the CGTD and register annually in the Plataforma de Cumplimiento Digital. Non-compliance carries disciplinary responsibility, and models, datasets and metadata are published in the Catálogo IA Perú.

Directiva 002-2026-PCM/SGTD, published 2026-10-21, defines the OIA's profile and duties. Its first final provision makes the article 9 profile requirement enforceable twelve months after entry into force, under the maximum administrative authority's responsibility. Article 2 obliges entities that hire third-party AI services to guarantee the security and data protection those third parties receive. This is the rule that stops an office from quietly adopting an unvetted chatbot.

## 3. Personal data and the authority's name

Ley 29733 protects personal data and grants access, rectification, cancellation and opposition rights. Its current regulation, DS 016-2024-JUS, replaced DS 003-2013-JUS and covers consent, transfers, outsourced processing, breach notification, the Data Protection Officer, impact assessment, security controls and the ARCO procedure.

The authority is the same body under two names: DS 115-2025-PCM calls it ANPDP, while gob.pe brands it ANPD, both within MINJUSDH. Cite whichever the source you are quoting uses, but do not treat them as two organisations.

No AI-specific ANPD sanction and no Peru-specific Contraloría guide for public servants were found. The available AI items from the authority are guidance documents, not rulings. Colombia's CGR Circular 023-2025 is not Peruvian law.

## 4. What an office worker may and may not do

| Do not | Do |
|---|---|
| Paste reserved or classified information, citizen or colleague personal data, or sensitive data into a public AI tool. | Check the entity's AI policy, Plan de Gobierno Digital and OIA before changing how a report is produced. |
| Let a system decide selections, benefits, credit, health or discipline. | Minimise and anonymise data before any AI-assisted processing. |
| Present AI output as a verified fact or a citable source. | Verify every figure and citation against the primary source and record the check. |
| Copy third-party text or images without rights clearance. | Keep the human review able to stop, correct or invalidate the output. |
| Assume an internal draft is exempt from labelling without checking whether it affects rights or services. | Label public-facing AI content, and keep drafts, prompts, source lists and review evidence where the entity requires traceability. |

## 5. Why the evidence model fits this work

Odachi's proof-driven discipline translates to document work without any software-development vocabulary. The mapping is a proposal, not current product behavior:

| Odachi idea | Office equivalent |
|---|---|
| Brief | The written statement of what the report must answer and what it must not contain. |
| Requirement quoted verbatim | The exact sentence the requester wrote, kept unchanged in the task document. |
| Proof tied to the tree | The exact command output or primary-source excerpt that supports the claim, saved with the document version it supports. |
| Review by a named lens | A second person or an independent pass that checks the claims, with the finding recorded. |
| Git without GitHub | A local repository that records which version was reviewed and which evidence belongs to it; no remote service is required. |

The last row matters for the intended audience. These workers do not need GitHub, a pull request or a CI runner. They need a local history that answers "which draft did we verify, and with what evidence?" — the same question odachi's proof answers for code.

## Gaps, limits and what was not verified

- The exact entry-into-force date of DS 016-2024-JUS is reported as 2025-03-31 by secondary sources but was not recalculated from the official calendar.
- The 48-hour breach-notification window often quoted for DS 016-2024-JUS was not confirmed from the primary text and is not relied on here.
- Ley 27806 on transparency and Ley 29608 on the national archive are cited in official texts, but their consolidated decree numbers were not re-verified here.
- Web search was degraded during this research (bot challenges and truncated queries). Norms with a `busquedas.elperuano.pe` or `gob.pe` link above were read directly; the rest come from official listings.
- This document does not design the `office peru` profile or its skill. It only establishes the evidence base for that decision.
